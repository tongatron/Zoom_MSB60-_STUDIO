    Help us test the MS-50G+

    The MS-50G+ pages of this site are built from the official ZOOM documents and from the file format of the MS-60B+, which has been checked on a real pedal. Nobody has tried a patch made here on a real MS-50G+ yet, so we are looking for someone who owns one and would like to help.

    This page lists what we would need, from the shortest check to the longest. Nobody is expected to do it all. If you can spare some time, write to us and tell us how much: this way we organise the tests together, according to the time you have, and we agree on how to send the results.

    The button opens a form: the message goes straight to the people who make this site. Add your email address, so we can answer.

    The short version: the preparation and tests 1 to 3 would take about 50 minutes, everything together about 4 hours. Any part helps. The times are estimates for someone who has not used the app much; the later tests go faster once the steps are known.

    What it would take

    On your side we would need

    • The MS-50G+, switched on, and an iPhone or iPad with the free Handy Guitar Lab for MS-50G+ app (App Store), connected to the pedal with a USB data cable. The Handy Guitar Lab guide (PDF) says which cable and adapter your device needs.
    • A computer, or an iPad held sideways, to open this site and download the test patches, and a way to move a file to the iPhone or iPad (AirDrop, Mail or Files).
    • An email address to send the files from.

    Looking after your patches

    • Copying a patch to the pedal overwrites that memory. A backup of all the memories first (the back-up pages of the Handy Guitar Lab guide show how, and how to restore it) and free memories for the tests would keep your own patches safe.
    • It is best to leave the pedal's knobs alone while the app is connected: on the MS-60B+ the pedal saved by itself what had been touched.

    We would also like to know

    • The firmware version of the pedal: on the pedal, select VERSION on the MENU screen.
    • The version of the Handy Guitar Lab app, and the model and system version of the iPhone or iPad.

    Test 1: a generated patch on the pedal

    About 15 minutes. We would need to know whether the app and the pedal accept a patch made on this site, and whether its values arrive as they were set. The values of the test patch are unusual on purpose, so that a value that ends up in the wrong place is easy to see.

    What it involves. Opening the TEST A patch in the Patch builder (the builder shows a DSP load of about 39.6%), downloading it as a .zptc file, importing it in Handy Guitar Lab and copying it to a free memory of the pedal, as described in How to load it on the pedal. The patch holds a compressor, an amp and a delay:

    EffectValues
    COMPRESSORSENSE 6, ATTCK FAST, TONE 5, VOL 50
    MS1959 DRIVEIN1 30, IN2 70, VOL 60, BASS 40, MID 55, TREBLE 65, PRESENCE 45
    DELAYTIME 350, MODE LONG, REPEAT 30, MIX 25

    What we would need to know

    • Whether the app imported the file, and the exact message if it showed one.
    • Whether the three effects are there, in this order, with the values above.
    • Whether the pedal shows the same effects and values, and which value is different if one is.
    • The processor or DSP percentage the app shows for the patch.
    • Whether it sounds as it should (no silence, noise or clicks).

    Screenshots of the app and a photo of the pedal's display would help a lot.

    Test 2: a patch made in the app

    About 10 minutes. We would need a patch file written by the app itself, to see how it stores a patch. Nothing from this site is involved.

    What it involves. Adding one COMPRESSOR to a free memory, leaving its values as they are, naming the patch TEST COMP, saving it, and sharing it from the Patch Memory screen (Select, tick the memory, Share; the Handy Guitar Lab guide shows the steps under "Sharing a Patch Memory").

    What we would need back: the .zptc file and the four values the app shows for the compressor (SENSE, ATTCK, TONE and VOL).

    Test 3: six effects in one patch

    About 15 minutes. On the MS-60B+ a six-effect patch written over USB lost its last effect on the pedal, because the pedal holds six entries. We would need to know whether all six effects of a patch from the builder reach the MS-50G+ through the app.

    What it involves. Opening the TEST C patch in the Patch builder (COMPRESSOR, TS DRIVE, MS1959 DRIVE, GUITAR GRAPHIC EQ7, CLONE CHORUS and HALL REVERB, with their default values; the builder shows a DSP load of about 68.2%), loading it on the pedal as in test 1 and stepping through its effects on the pedal, from the first to the last.

    What we would need to know

    • How many effects the app shows for the patch after the copy, and which ones.
    • How many effects can be reached on the pedal, and whether the sixth, HALL REVERB, is there.
    • The processor or DSP percentage the app shows.
    • Whether the app or the pedal showed any message.

    Test 4: parametric EQ values

    About 15 minutes. The builder cannot write the FREQ and Q of the PARAMETRIC EQ yet, because we do not know how the pedal stores them. On the MS-60B+, which has the same ranges, Q grows in steps of 0.1 from 0.5 (checked up to 3.2) and FREQ does not grow in even steps. We would need to see whether the MS-50G+ does the same.

    What it involves. Six patches in free memories, each with a single PARAMETRIC EQ (GAIN and VOL left as they are) set to the FREQ and Q of the table, named after the values the app shows (for example PEQ 50Hz 3.2) and shared together. If the app cannot show exactly a value of the table, the nearest one will do, with the patch named after what it really shows.

    What we would need back: the six files.

    Test 7: amp models

    About 3 minutes per amp. Amps carry data that ZOOM does not document: on the MS-60B+ every amp has a hidden ninth value and a flag of its own in the patch header. We would like to know whether the MS-50G+ does the same.

    What it involves. A patch with only that amp, its values left as they are, named after the amp and shared. MS1959 DRIVE, FD TWIN-R DRIVE and RECTI DUAL DRIVE come first (about 10 minutes); the more amps the better.

    The 22 amp models: MS45OS DRIVE, MS1959 DRIVE, MS800 DRIVE, FD B-MAN DRIVE, FD TWIN-R DRIVE, FD DELUXE-R DRIVE, FD MASTER DRIVE, UK 30A DRIVE, BG MARK1 DRIVE, BG MARK3 DRIVE, RECTI DUAL DRIVE, XTASY BLUE DRIVE, HW 100 DRIVE, ORG120 DRIVE, DZ DRIVE, MATCH30 DRIVE, KRAMPUS DRIVE, REDLOOM DRIVE, VELVET DRIVE, MUDDY DRIVE, 7 HEAVEN DRIVE, POLLEX DRIVE.

    Test 8: add-on effects

    About 3 minutes per effect. The effects marked with a star in the ZOOM Effects List are added to the pedal with Handy Guitar Lab, and the builder does not know their identifiers yet, so it cannot use them.

    What it involves. For each add-on effect that is installed: a patch with only that effect, its values left as they are, named after the effect and shared.

    The 24 add-on effects: OPTICAL COMPRESSOR, MUTE SWITCH, RESONANCE FILTER, CRY FILTER, LFO FILTER, RANDOM FILTER, GUITAR GRAPHIC EQ, STEREO GUITAR GRAPHIC EQ, EXCITER, LOW EQ, HIGH EQ, BG GRID DRIVE, METAL WORLD DRIVE, TS+BOOST DRIVE, RED CRUNCH DRIVE + BOOST, SPOT BOOSTER, UP OCTAVE BOOSTER, OUTPUT BOOSTER, CHORUS, BEND CHORUS, DUO PHASER, PHASE DELAY, AUTO PAN DELAY, SLOW ATTACK DELAY.
