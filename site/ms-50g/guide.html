    Guide

    How to use ZOOM MultiStomp Studio with the ZOOM MS-50G+, from finding an effect to playing the patch. There is nothing to install and you do not need an account to start. The site is made for a computer or a tablet held sideways: it needs a window of at least 800 × 640 px, and below that it shows a notice instead (try the full-screen mode of the browser, F11).

    Not tested yet. This section is built from the official MS-50G+ documentation and the same file format as the MS-60B+, but no generated patch has been checked on a real MS-50G+. Own the pedal? Help us test it: a few short tests at home, with the time each one takes.

    Quick start

    1. Build the chain. In the Patch builder pick up to 6 effects, put them in order and set every parameter with the real ranges.
    2. Check it. Watch the DSP load against the 100% limit and read the advice before you leave the desk.
    3. Send it to the pedal. Download the .zptc file and load it with Handy Guitar Lab (how to do it).

    Find an effect

    The Effects page lists all 127 effects with the original text of the ZOOM Effects List.

    • Type in the search box at the top of the list, or press /: it looks in names, models, descriptions and parameter names.
    • Narrow the list with the Categories and Features filters on the left, change the order with the sort menu, and switch between list and grid with the two buttons above the list.
    • Click an effect to see it in the pane on the right: description, placement tips, estimated DSP load and every parameter with its range. ← → move to the previous or next effect.
    • Add to patch, right under the description in the detail, opens the Patch builder with that effect in the chain.
    • 24 of the effects are Handy Guitar Lab add-ons (★ in the Effects List): you can read them here, but the builder cannot use them yet.

    Build a patch

    The Patch builder works like the pedal: the signal flows from INPUT, at the top of the chain, to OUTPUT, at the bottom.

    • Add effect opens the effect picker: search by name or description, or browse by category. A patch holds up to 6 effects; the counter next to Chain shows how many you use.
    • Each effect has an on/off switch and buttons to move it towards the input or the output, replace it with another effect and remove it. You can also drag the grip on its left to reorder.
    • Click an effect's name, or its arrow, to show its parameters. Sliders and menus use the real ranges of the pedal.
    • The DSP load bar is an estimate on the same scale as the "Processor" meter of Handy Guitar Lab. Above 100% the pedal shows PROCESS OVERFLOW and bypasses the effects, so Download .zptc stays disabled until the patch fits.
    • The patch name takes up to 28 characters: letters, digits, spaces and ! % & ' + , - . = _.
    • Undo and Redo bring back earlier steps (Ctrl/⌘ + Z; add Shift to redo).

    Read the advice

    The Advice pane checks the chain while you build it. Errors and warnings point at things that would not work or would sound wrong; tips are conventions you can follow or not. Every item has a Why link with the reasoning and its sources.

    • The Errors, Warnings and Tips buttons show or hide each kind.
    • Ignore hides an item you chose on purpose; Show again, at the bottom, brings the ignored ones back. My preferences pins choices you make on purpose.
    • Plug into (Amp, or Mixer / PA / headphones) turns the cabinet advice into an exact check.
    • On the MS-50G+ the advice makes basic checks only (levels and DSP load).
    • Rule packs add your own rules from a .json file; every rule must cite a source.

    The alerts at the top of the pane are about the patch itself:

    • Check on the pedal lists parameters whose encoding was inferred by analogy ("unverified"): after loading the patch, check their values on the pedal. Point at the i icon beside the notice to see why.
    • Raw values (format not decoded) lists parameters where the builder asks for the stored value ("raw") instead of the value shown on the pedal. The i icon beside the notice explains why.
    • Reminders such as Give the patch a name, and the messages after an import or a copied link, appear here too.

    Save and compare

    • Save (Ctrl/⌘ + S) puts the patch in My patches; Save as new saves a copy and leaves the original as it is.
    • Click a patch in My patches to open it. Create new patch starts an empty one.
    • Compare puts two patches side by side.
    • Backup downloads all your saved patches as a .json file. Import reads .zptc files, for example from Handy Guitar Lab, and .json backups; you can also drop the files onto the page.

    How to load it on the pedal

    1. In Handy Guitar Lab open Patch, tap the import icon and pick the .zptc file: it lands in the Pool.
    2. In the Pool tap Select, choose the patch and tap Copy to Device.
    3. Pick a free memory: copying overwrites the destination patch.

    Get the file with Download .zptc in the Patch builder. More in the Handy Guitar Lab guide (PDF).

    Keep your patches safe

    You can use everything without signing in, but then your work lives only in one browser: clearing browsing data or using a private window loses it, and another browser or device won't have it.

    • A free account (Sign in, top right) keeps your patches, settings, rule packs and pedal backups on the server and brings them to every device where you sign in. The patches already in your browser come with you when you sign up.
    • When you open the studio without an account, a message reminds you of this, with the links to Create a free account and Sign in. Maybe later shows it again the next time you visit; Ignore this message hides it for good in that browser.
    • Signing out removes your data from that browser by default; it stays in your account.
    • On the account page you can download everything or delete the account. Privacy says exactly what is stored.

    Troubleshooting

    • Download .zptc is greyed out. The patch needs at least one effect, a name made of the allowed characters, and an estimated DSP load of 100% or less.
    • The pedal shows PROCESS OVERFLOW. The patch asks for more than the pedal can process. Swap or remove a heavy effect: sort the Effects list by DSP: lightest to find lighter ones.
    • My patches are gone. They are kept in the browser: another browser or computer, a private window or cleared site data does not have them. Signed in, they come back from your account; otherwise import a .json backup.
    • The page says the window is too small. The site needs a window of at least 800 × 640 px: make it bigger or switch the browser to full screen (F11).
    • Something is wrong, or you miss something. Press Report a problem in the bar at the bottom of any page and write a message: it goes straight to the person who makes the site. Add your email if you would like an answer.

    Words used here

    • Patch: up to 6 effects in a chain, with their settings and a name of up to 28 characters.
    • DSP load: how much of the pedal's processing power a patch uses. Above 100% the pedal bypasses the effects.
    • .zptc: the patch file that Handy Guitar Lab, the ZOOM app for editing patches, reads and writes.
